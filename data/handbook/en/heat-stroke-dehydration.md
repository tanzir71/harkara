# Heat stroke, heat exhaustion and dehydration

**Call 999 first if** a person in the heat is confused, faints, has a seizure, or cannot keep fluids down. Heat stroke kills. Start cooling at once, before help arrives.

Heat waves, humidity, power cuts and crowded rooms put babies, older people, outdoor workers, pregnant women and people with long-term illness most at risk.

## Heat exhaustion

Signs: heavy sweating, cold clammy skin, tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, muscle cramps, thirst, fast pulse.

1. Move them to a cool, shaded place with moving air.
2. Lay them down and raise their legs.
3. Remove extra clothing.
4. If they are alert, give water or ORS to sip.
5. Wet their skin and fan them.
6. They should improve within 30 minutes. If not, treat as heat stroke.

## Heat stroke

Signs: very hot body; hot, red skin, dry or sweating; confusion, slurred speech or strange behaviour; fainting, seizure or collapse.

1. Call 999.
2. Move them to the coolest place you can.
3. **Cool them fast.** Best: put the body in cool water up to the neck (a tub or large drum), with someone holding the head. Otherwise soak them all over with water and fan hard. Put wet cloths or ice packs on the neck, armpits and groin.
4. Keep cooling until they are alert or help arrives.
5. If fully awake, give cool water to sip.
6. If unresponsive, use the recovery position. Start CPR if they stop breathing normally.

**Do not give paracetamol or aspirin for heat stroke.** It does not bring this temperature down.

## Dehydration

Signs: thirst, dry mouth, little or dark urine, tiredness, dizziness. In babies: sunken soft spot on the head, few wet nappies, no tears, unusual sleepiness. Drink small amounts often; use ORS for diarrhoea, vomiting or heavy sweating.

## ORS (oral rehydration solution)

**Best:** a packet of ORS from a pharmacy, mixed exactly as the packet says. Most packets sold in Bangladesh are for **half a litre (500 ml)** of water.

**WHO home recipe, if you have no packet:**

- 1 litre of clean, boiled and cooled drinking water
- 6 level teaspoons of sugar
- 1/2 level teaspoon of salt

Stir until dissolved. Measure carefully: too much salt is dangerous, especially for babies. Make it fresh every day and throw away leftovers after 24 hours. Give small sips often, by spoon for small children.

## Prevent heat illness

- Drink water through the day, before you feel thirsty.
- Avoid hard outdoor work from late morning to mid-afternoon.
- Wear loose, light cotton clothes and cover your head.
- Check on older neighbours and people living alone.
- **Never leave a child or anyone in a parked car**, even for a few minutes.
- In extreme heat a fan alone may not be enough. Wet the skin and take cool showers.
