# Head injury

**Call 999 first if** the person was knocked out even briefly, is confused or drowsy, vomits more than once, has a seizure, has blood or clear fluid from the nose or ears, has weakness on one side, or was hurt in a fall from height or a road accident.

## What to do

1. Check DRSABC. If they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR.
2. If unresponsive but breathing, put them in the recovery position, keeping the head and neck in line.
3. Suspect a neck injury with any serious head injury. Move them only if you must.
4. For a scalp wound, press firmly with a clean cloth. Scalp wounds bleed a lot.
5. Put a cold pack wrapped in cloth on a bump for up to 20 minutes.
6. Keep them still and calm. Stay with them.

## Watch for 48 hours

Bleeding inside the skull can start hours after a bump that looked mild. An adult should stay with the person for at least the first 24 hours. Go to hospital at once if:

- The headache gets worse or does not go away.
- They vomit more than once.
- They become very sleepy, confused or hard to wake.
- Speech is slurred, or they have weakness, numbness or trouble walking.
- They have a seizure, or act strangely.
- Clear fluid comes from the nose or ears.

The first night, let them sleep, but check on them every few hours: they should wake easily and know where they are.

## Higher risk

Take any head injury seriously in babies and young children, older people, people who have been drinking, and anyone on blood-thinning medicine.

A child who cries straight away and then plays normally is usually fine. Get help if they vomit repeatedly, are unusually sleepy or floppy, or have a soft swelling on the head.

## Do not

- **Do not give alcohol, sleeping pills or strong painkillers.** Paracetamol is fine for a mild headache.
- **Do not leave them alone** in the first hours.
- **Do not let them play sport, cycle or drive** that day.
- **Do not take off a motorbike helmet** unless you must, to open the airway.
