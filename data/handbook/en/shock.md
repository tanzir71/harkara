# Shock

**Call 999 first.** Shock means the body is not getting enough blood flow. It is a medical emergency and can kill.

Shock can follow heavy bleeding, large burns, severe diarrhoea or vomiting, a severe allergy, a heart attack or a serious infection. It is not the same as being emotionally upset.

## Signs

- Pale or grey skin, cold and clammy to touch.
- Fast, weak pulse; fast, shallow breathing.
- Sweating, thirst, nausea.
- Weakness, dizziness, anxiety or confusion.
- Later: drowsiness and loss of response.

## What to do

1. Treat the cause if you can. Stop any bleeding first.
2. Lay the person down flat.
3. Raise their legs about 30 cm, on a bag or chair, **if it does not hurt them** and the legs, hips, back and head are not injured.
4. Loosen tight clothing at the neck, chest and waist.
5. Keep them warm with a blanket or clothes, under and over them. Do not overheat them.
6. Reassure them and stay with them.
7. Check breathing and response every few minutes. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.

## Special cases

- **Difficulty breathing:** let them sit in the position they find easiest instead of lying flat.
- **Pregnant woman:** lay her on her left side.
- **Severe allergy:** use their adrenaline auto-injector if they have one. See "Allergic reactions and anaphylaxis".
- **Shock from diarrhoea or cholera:** fluid is the treatment. If they are awake and can swallow, give ORS in sips while you get them to hospital urgently.

## Do not

- **Do not give food or drink** after an injury, burn or bleeding. They may need surgery or may vomit. Wet their lips if they are very thirsty.
- **Do not let them get up or walk around.**
- **Do not leave them alone.**
