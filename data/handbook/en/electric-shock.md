# Electric shock and lightning

**Call 999 first if** the shock was from high voltage or lightning, or the person is unresponsive, burnt, short of breath, has chest pain or an irregular heartbeat.

## Make it safe first

1. **Do not touch the person while they are still in contact with the current.** You will be shocked too.
2. Switch off the power at the main switch or circuit breaker, or pull out the plug.
3. If you cannot switch off household power, stand on something dry that does not conduct (a wooden board, rubber mat or thick dry newspaper) and push the source away with a dry wooden stick, broom handle or plastic chair. **Never use anything wet or metal.**
4. **High voltage** (overhead lines, transformers, substations): stay at least 18 metres away and keep others back. Call 999 and wait until the line is switched off.
5. If a live wire is in water, do not enter the water.

## Then give first aid

1. Check response and breathing.
2. If not breathing normally, start CPR. Use an AED if one is available.
3. If breathing, put them in the recovery position.
4. Once the power is off, cool burns under running water for 20 minutes. There are often burns where the current entered and left the body.
5. Watch for shock.

Everyone who has had an electric shock should see a doctor, even if they feel fine. Electricity can upset the heart rhythm and cause deep burns that do not show.

## Lightning

Lightning kills many people in Bangladesh every year, mostly from April to June, often farmers and people outdoors.

- A person struck by lightning does not carry a charge. Touch them and help at once.
- If several people are hit, help first those who are not moving or not breathing. Start CPR.
- When you hear thunder, go indoors into a solid building. Stay off open fields, rooftops, boats and water, away from lone trees, towers and metal poles.
- If you are caught in the open, crouch low on the balls of your feet with your feet together, head down and ears covered. Do not lie flat.

## Prevent shocks

- Do not touch switches or plugs with wet hands.
- Do not overload sockets with multi-plugs.
- Do not hang wet clothes on wires or near meters.
- Repair loose wiring and cover open switchboards.
- Keep children away from sockets and loose wires.
- In waterlogged streets, keep away from electric poles, lamp posts and fallen wires.
