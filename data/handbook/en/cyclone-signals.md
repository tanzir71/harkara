# Cyclone warning signals

**Call 999 first if** you are in danger during a storm. On the coast or a char, do what Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP) volunteers and officials tell you, at once.

The Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) issues numbered signals. **The number shows the danger at a particular port, not the size of the storm.** A strong cyclone far away can show a low number, and signals can jump, for example from 4 straight to 7 or 10.

## Sea port signals

For the sea ports of Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Mongla and Payra, and nearby coastal areas.

- **1, Distant cautionary:** squally weather far out at sea, where a storm may form. Ships leaving port may meet bad weather.
- **2, Distant warning:** a storm has formed in the deep sea. The port is not in danger yet, but ships leaving it may be.
- **3, Local cautionary:** the port may get squally weather, with wind of about 40 to 50 km/h.
- **4, Local warning:** the port is threatened by a storm, wind about 51 to 61 km/h, but final precautions are not yet needed.
- **5, 6, 7, Danger:** a storm with wind of 62 to 88 km/h will hit the port. 5 and 6 mean it crosses the coast on one side of the port or the other; 7 means it crosses over or near the port.
- **8, 9, 10, Great danger:** a severe cyclone with wind of 89 km/h or more will hit the port. 8 and 9 mean it crosses on one side or the other; 10 means over or near the port.
- **11, Failure of communication:** contact with the warning centre is lost, and local officials think the weather is extremely dangerous.

## CPP flags and announcements on the coast

CPP volunteers pass on warnings by word of mouth, megaphone, loudspeaker, flags and siren.

- **Signals 1 to 3:** no flag. Volunteers spread the word. Get ready and follow the radio and TV.
- **Signal 4:** **1 flag**. Be alert and know your nearest shelter.
- **Signals 5 to 7:** **2 flags**. Danger. Secure your house, move vulnerable people, get ready to leave.
- **Signals 8 to 10:** **3 flags** and a hand siren. Great danger. Go to the cyclone shelter now.

## River port signals

Inland river ports, launches and boats have 4 separate signals. **River signal 4 is not the same as sea signal 4.**

- **1, Cautionary:** brief squally weather or a nor'wester (kalbaishakhi) of up to about 60 km/h may hit. Watch the weather.
- **2, Warning:** a storm, or a nor'wester of 61 km/h or more, may hit. Vessels 65 feet long or shorter must go to shelter quickly.
- **3, Danger:** a storm of 62 to 88 km/h may hit soon. All vessels must take shelter at once.
- **4, Great danger:** a severe storm of 89 km/h or more will hit soon. All vessels must stay in shelter.

## What this means in Dhaka

- Dhaka is inland, but cyclones bring heavy rain, waterlogging, strong wind, and fallen trees and wires.
- Check the river port signal before travelling by launch from Sadarghat or any river terminal. Do not travel when a danger signal is up.
- Tie down loose roof sheets, flower pots and signboards, and stay indoors in strong wind.

## Where to hear the signals

Bangladesh Betar radio, TV, the free **1090** line, BMD, CPP volunteers, local announcements, and Harkara Alert posts from neighbours. Trust official sources over forwarded messages.
