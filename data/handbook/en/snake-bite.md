# Snake bite

**Call 999 first, and go as fast as possible to a government hospital that has antivenom.** Treat every bite as venomous until a doctor says otherwise.

Bangladesh has several deadly snakes, including cobras, kraits and Russell's viper. Floods push snakes into homes and onto high ground. Kraits often bite at night while people sleep on the floor; the bite may hardly hurt, and the person may wake with stomach pain, weakness or drooping eyelids.

## What to do

1. Move away from the snake. **Do not try to catch or kill it.** If it is safe, take a photo from a distance.
2. Keep the person calm and still. Movement spreads venom faster.
3. Remove rings, bangles, anklets, watches and tight clothing near the bite before it swells.
4. **Keep the bitten limb still** with a splint (stick, board or rolled newspaper) and loose cloth ties, as for a broken bone.
5. Carry the person, or take them by vehicle, rickshaw or boat. They should not walk.
6. Go straight to a hospital with antivenom: a medical college hospital or district hospital; many upazila health complexes also have it. Phone ahead if you can.
7. Note the time of the bite and any changes on the way.

## Danger signs

Drooping eyelids, double vision, trouble swallowing or speaking, weakness, difficulty breathing, bleeding from the gums or the bite, red or dark urine, vomiting or stomach pain.

If breathing stops, give rescue breaths and CPR. People paralysed by snake venom can survive if someone keeps breathing for them until they reach hospital.

## Do not

- **Do not tie a tourniquet or a tight band.** It does not stop the venom and can destroy the limb.
- **Do not cut, burn or suck the bite.**
- **Do not put herbs, stones, chemicals, ice or cow dung on it.**
- **Do not go to an ojha (traditional snake healer) or wait for local treatment.** Time lost before antivenom is a main reason people die.
- **Do not give alcohol, aspirin or ibuprofen.** Paracetamol is fine for pain.

## Prevent bites

- Sleep on a raised bed, under a mosquito net tucked in well. This protects against kraits.
- Use a torch at night and wear shoes outdoors.
- Keep the house and yard clear of rubbish, firewood piles and rat holes; rats attract snakes.
- Look before you put your hand into holes, straw or under stones.
- In floods, watch where you step, and check bedding and shoes before use.
