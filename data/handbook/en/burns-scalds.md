# Burns and scalds

**Call 999 first if** the burn is larger than the person's hand, deep, on the face, hands, feet, genitals or a joint, goes all the way round a limb, is from electricity or chemicals, or the person has breathed in smoke.

## Stop the burning

1. Move the person away from the heat.
2. If clothes are on fire: **stop, drop and roll**, or smother the flames with a heavy cotton or wool cloth.
3. Switch off the power before touching someone with an electrical burn.

## Cool the burn

1. Cool the burn under **cool running water for 20 minutes**. Start as soon as possible; cooling still helps up to 3 hours later.
2. Use a tap, bucket or bottle. Clean drinking water is best; pond or flood water can infect the burn.
3. Take off rings, bangles, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells.
4. Keep the rest of the body warm. Babies and older people get cold quickly.

## Cover the burn

1. Lay cling film (plastic food wrap) loosely along the burn, or put a clean plastic bag over a burnt hand.
2. If you have nothing else, use a clean cloth that does not shed fluff.
3. **Do not wrap cling film tightly round a limb.**

## Chemical burns

1. Protect your own hands.
2. Brush off dry powder first.
3. Rinse with plenty of running water for at least 20 minutes, removing soaked clothing as you rinse.
4. Note the name of the chemical for the doctor.

Acid thrown on a person is a chemical burn: pour water at once, and keep pouring on the way to hospital.

## Smoke breathed in

Signs: burns or soot around the nose or mouth, hoarse voice, coughing, difficulty breathing. Call 999. Help them sit up and watch their breathing.

## Do not

- **Do not use ice or ice water.** It damages the skin more.
- **Do not put toothpaste, butter, oil, ghee, egg, honey, turmeric or ointment** on a burn.
- **Do not burst blisters.**
- **Do not pull off clothing stuck to the burn.** Cut around it.

## Afterwards

- Paracetamol can ease the pain once the burn is cooled.
- See a doctor for any burn on a baby or young child, any blistered burn larger than a coin, any white, brown or black skin, or later signs of infection: more pain, redness, pus or fever.

## Prevent scalds

Hot tea, rice water and cooking oil scald many children. Keep children out of the cooking area, turn pot handles inward, and keep hot drinks out of reach.
