# Choking: adults and children over 1

**Call 999 first if** the person cannot breathe, cough or speak, or becomes unresponsive.

## Mild choking: they can cough

- Tell them to keep coughing. Coughing is the best way to clear the airway.
- Stay with them. Do not slap their back while they are coughing well.
- If the cough becomes weak or silent, treat it as severe choking.

## Severe choking: they cannot cough, speak or breathe

Signs: hands at the throat, silent attempts to cough, lips turning blue, panic.

1. Ask: "Are you choking?" If they nod and cannot speak, act now.
2. **Up to 5 back blows.** Stand to the side and slightly behind. Support the chest with one hand and lean them well forward. Strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. Check after each blow.
3. **Up to 5 abdominal thrusts.** Stand behind them and put your arms round their waist. Make a fist and place it between the belly button and the bottom of the breastbone. Grasp it with your other hand. Pull sharply inwards and upwards.
4. Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.

For a small child, kneel behind them so you are at their height.

## If they become unresponsive

1. Lower them carefully to the floor.
2. Make sure 999 has been called.
3. Start CPR. Each time you open the airway, look in the mouth and remove any object you can see.

## Special cases

- **Pregnant or very large person:** give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts. Put your fist on the lower half of the breastbone and pull sharply straight back.
- **Alone and choking:** thrust your upper belly hard against the back of a chair, table edge or railing. Call 999 or send a Harkara SOS if you can.

## Afterwards

Anyone who had abdominal thrusts should be checked by a doctor. The thrusts can cause internal injury.

## Do not

- **Do not put your fingers down the throat blindly.** You can push the object deeper.
- **Do not give water** to wash it down.
- **Do not give abdominal thrusts to a baby under 1.** See "Choking: babies".

## Prevention

- Cut food for young children into small pieces. Grapes, nuts, hard sweets, chickpeas and fish bones are common risks.
- Children should sit to eat, not run, play or lie down.
- Keep coins, button batteries, bottle caps and balloons away from small children.
