# Choking: babies under 1 year

**Call 999 first if** the baby cannot cry, cough or breathe, or becomes unresponsive. If you are alone, give one round of back blows and chest thrusts first, then call, taking the baby with you.

## Signs

- Sudden silence, or weak, high-pitched noises.
- Unable to cry or cough.
- Face turning red, then pale or blue.

If the baby is coughing strongly or crying, let them cough and watch closely.

## Back blows

1. Sit or kneel. Lay the baby face down along your forearm, with your arm resting on your thigh.
2. Support the head by holding the jaw between your thumb and fingers. **Do not press on the throat.**
3. Keep the head lower than the chest.
4. Give up to **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
5. Check after each blow whether the object has come out.

## Chest thrusts

1. Turn the baby face up along your other forearm, supporting the back of the head. Keep the head low.
2. Put **two fingertips** in the centre of the chest, just below the nipple line.
3. Give up to **5 chest thrusts**, pressing down about 4 cm. They are slower and sharper than CPR compressions.
4. Check the mouth after each thrust. Remove an object only if you can see it and pick it out easily.

## Repeat

Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until the object comes out or the baby becomes unresponsive.

## If the baby becomes unresponsive

1. Lay the baby on a firm, flat surface. Call 999.
2. Start baby CPR: 5 rescue breaths, then 30 compressions with two fingers and 2 breaths. See "CPR for children and babies".
3. Look in the mouth each time before giving breaths.

## Do not

- **Do not give abdominal thrusts to a baby.** They can injure the liver and other organs.
- **Do not sweep blindly with your finger** in the baby's mouth.
- **Do not hold the baby upside down by the legs and shake them.**

## Afterwards

Take the baby to a doctor, even if the object came out and they seem fine.

## Prevention

- Keep small objects off the floor: coins, beads, buttons, button batteries, seeds and nuts.
- Never leave a baby alone with a propped-up feeding bottle.
- Give soft, mashed food until the baby can chew.
