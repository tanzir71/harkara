# Severe bleeding

**Call 999 first if** blood is spurting or flowing fast, soaking through cloth, or the person is pale, cold, confused or faint.

Heavy bleeding can kill in minutes. Stopping it comes before almost everything else.

## Stop the bleeding

1. Put on gloves, or plastic bags over your hands, if you have them. Do not let this delay you.
2. Help the person sit or lie down.
3. Find where the blood is coming from. Cut or tear clothing away if needed.
4. Press hard, directly on the wound, with a clean cloth, gauze or your hand.
5. Keep pressing firmly for **at least 10 minutes** without lifting to look.
6. If blood soaks through, put more cloth on top and press harder. **Do not remove the first cloth.**
7. When bleeding slows, tie a bandage or strip of cloth firmly over the pad. Fingers or toes beyond it should stay warm and pink.

## Object stuck in the wound

- **Do not pull it out.** It may be plugging the bleeding.
- Press on both sides of the object, not on it.
- Pad around it with rolled cloth and bandage over the padding.

## Tourniquet (arm or leg, life-threatening bleeding only)

If bleeding from a limb does not stop with firm pressure and you have a tourniquet, or are trained:

1. Place it 5 to 7 cm above the wound, not on a joint.
2. Tighten until the bleeding stops. It will hurt.
3. Note the time and tell the ambulance or hospital.
4. **Do not loosen it once it is on.** Leave that to doctors.

If you must improvise, use a wide strip of cloth (at least 4 cm) and twist it tight with a stick. A thin rope or wire cuts the skin and does not work.

## Watch for shock

Pale, cold, sweaty skin, fast breathing, thirst or confusion. Lay them down, raise the legs if they are not injured, and keep them warm. See "Shock".

## Nosebleed

1. Sit up and lean **forward**, not back.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 to 15 minutes without letting go.
3. Spit out blood rather than swallowing it.
4. Get medical help if it lasts over 20 minutes, follows a head injury, or the person takes blood-thinning medicine.

## Small cuts

1. Rinse under clean running water.
2. Press with a clean cloth until it stops.
3. Cover with a clean dressing.
4. Ask about a tetanus injection for dirty wounds, rusty metal, soil or animal bites.

## Do not

- **Do not put soil, ash, turmeric, toothpaste or chewed leaves on a wound.**
- **Do not give food or drink** to someone bleeding heavily. They may need surgery.
- Wash your hands well afterwards.
