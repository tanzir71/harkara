# Drowning

**Call 999 first.** Shout for help. Drowning is fast and often silent: a person in trouble usually cannot wave or call out.

Drowning is one of the biggest killers of young children in Bangladesh. Most happen close to home, in ponds, ditches, buckets and flood water, within minutes of a child going missing.

## Rescue safely

1. **Do not jump in** unless you are trained in water rescue. Many rescuers drown.
2. **Reach** with a long stick, pole, bamboo, dupatta or rope, while lying flat on the bank.
3. **Throw** something that floats: an empty plastic bottle or jerrycan, a banana stem, a tyre tube.
4. Go in only if the water is shallow and still, and hold on to something or someone on the bank.
5. Once they hold on, pull them to the edge.

## When they are out of the water

1. Check response and breathing.
2. **Not breathing normally:** call 999 and start CPR. Begin with **5 rescue breaths**, then 30 compressions and 2 breaths. Breaths matter most for drowning.
3. **Breathing:** put them in the recovery position. Remove wet clothes and keep them warm with dry cloth.
4. If they vomit, turn them on their side and clear the mouth, then continue.

## Everyone needs a hospital check

Anyone pulled from the water who was unconscious, needed breaths, or keeps coughing should go to hospital, even if they seem fine. The lungs can get worse hours later.

## Do not

- **Do not press on the stomach** to push water out. It does not help and can make them vomit into the lungs.
- **Do not hold them upside down, spin them or roll them over a pot or drum.**
- **Do not waste time** trying to drain water. Start breaths.

## Prevent drowning

- Watch young children near water all the time, with your eyes, not your phone.
- Cover water tanks, wells, drums and buckets. Empty tubs after use.
- Fence ponds and ditches near the house, or keep small children in a safe play area or daycare.
- Teach children over 4 to swim, with a trained instructor.
- Wear a life jacket on boats and launches. Do not board overcrowded boats.
- In floods, keep children on high ground, away from currents.
