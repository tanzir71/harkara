# After a flood

**Call 999 first if** a building looks unsafe, you smell gas, there are live wires, or someone is ill or injured.

## Going home

1. Go back only when officials say it is safe.
2. Look at the building from outside first: cracks, leaning walls, a sagging roof or floor. **Do not go in if it looks unsafe.**
3. If you smell gas, do not switch anything on or light a match. Leave and report it.
4. Have an electrician check the wiring before switching the power on. Do not use wet appliances until they are checked.
5. Open doors and windows to dry and air the house.

## Cleaning up

- Wear rubber boots and gloves. Cover any cuts.
- Shovel out mud. Wash floors, walls, furniture and utensils with clean water and detergent, then disinfect with household bleach diluted as the bottle says.
- **Never mix bleach with ammonia or acid cleaners.** It makes a poisonous gas.
- Throw away food that touched flood water, including opened packets and anything not in a sealed can.
- Dry bedding and clothes in the sun. Throw away mattresses and pillows you cannot dry completely.
- Clean and disinfect water tanks. Have flooded tubewells pumped out and disinfected, and repair cracked platforms.
- Wash your hands with soap after cleaning.

## Illness after floods

Watch for these in the weeks after the water goes down:

- **Diarrhoea and cholera:** start ORS early and go to hospital for danger signs.
- **Typhoid and hepatitis:** a fever that does not go away, or yellow eyes. See a doctor.
- **Skin and wound infections:** keep wounds clean, dry and covered. Ask about tetanus.
- **Leptospirosis:** fever, muscle pain (especially in the calves) and red eyes after wading in flood water. See a doctor.
- **Dengue and malaria:** mosquitoes breed in leftover water. Empty containers and use nets.
- **Coughs and pneumonia** in children and older people.
- **Snake bites** continue while water levels fall.

Keep boiling or treating drinking water until the authorities say the supply is safe.

## Mind and body

Floods are exhausting and frightening. Talk with people you trust. Keep children's routines as normal as you can. Check on neighbours who live alone. If someone talks about wanting to die, stay with them and help them get support.

## Help each other

- Share information about safe water, ORS and help points on Harkara Nearby.
- Report blocked drains, damaged wires and open manholes to the city corporation.
- Photograph damage and keep records for relief and repairs.
