# Check and call: DRSABC

**Call 999 first if** someone is unresponsive, not breathing normally, bleeding heavily, or badly hurt.

Use these six steps, in order, whenever you find someone hurt or collapsed. Remember them as D-R-S-A-B-C.

## D: Danger

1. Stop and look before you go near.
2. Check for traffic, fire, smoke, gas smell, live wires, deep or moving water, and anything that could fall.
3. Make the place safe if you can: switch off the power at the main switch, turn off the gas, ask others to stop traffic.
4. **Do not go in if it is not safe.** Call 999 and wait at a safe distance.

## R: Response

1. Kneel beside the person and tap their shoulders firmly.
2. Speak loudly: "Can you hear me? Open your eyes!"
3. If they answer or move, they are responsive. Ask what happened and look for injuries.
4. If they do not respond, they are unresponsive. Go on to S.

## S: Send for help

1. Shout for help from people nearby.
2. Ask one person to call 999 and then come back to you. If you call yourself, put the phone on speaker.
3. Ask someone to fetch a defibrillator (AED) if there may be one nearby, for example in a shopping mall, airport, hotel or large office.
4. If no call gets through, send a Harkara SOS.

## A: Airway

1. Put one hand on the forehead and gently tilt the head back.
2. Lift the chin with two fingers of your other hand.
3. Look in the mouth. Remove anything loose that you can see, with your fingers.
4. **Do not sweep blindly inside the mouth.** You may push an object deeper.

## B: Breathing

1. Keep the airway open. Put your cheek near their mouth and watch the chest.
2. Look, listen and feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
3. Occasional gasps are **not** normal breathing. Treat gasping as no breathing.
4. If they are breathing normally, put them in the recovery position (below) and keep checking.

## C: CPR

- If they are not breathing normally, start CPR straight away. See "CPR for adults" or "CPR for children and babies".
- Keep going until help arrives, the person starts breathing normally, or you are too exhausted to continue.

## Recovery position

For someone who is unresponsive but breathing normally.

1. Kneel beside them. Place the arm nearest you at a right angle to their body, palm up.
2. Bring the far arm across the chest and hold the back of that hand against their near cheek.
3. With your other hand, bend their far knee up, foot flat on the ground.
4. Pull on the bent knee to roll them towards you, onto their side.
5. Tilt the head back so the airway stays open. Adjust the hand under the cheek.
6. Check their breathing every minute until help arrives.

If you think the neck or back is injured, keep the head and neck in line with the body. Move them only if they are in danger or their airway is blocked.

## Then check the rest

Once breathing is dealt with, look for heavy bleeding, burns, broken bones and signs of shock. Stop heavy bleeding first.

## Do not

- **Do not move an injured person** unless they are in danger where they are.
- **Do not give food or drink** to someone who is drowsy or badly hurt.
- **Do not leave an unresponsive person alone** unless you must go to call for help.
