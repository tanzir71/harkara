# Broken bones, dislocations and sprains

**Call 999 first if** a bone is sticking out, a limb is bent the wrong way, there is heavy bleeding, the injury is to the head, neck, back, hip or thigh, or the person cannot move.

## Signs of a broken bone

- Pain, swelling and bruising.
- The limb looks bent, shortened or twisted.
- The person cannot use it or put weight on it.

If you are not sure, treat it as broken.

## What to do

1. Keep the person still. Tell them not to move the injured part.
2. Support the injury in the position you found it, with your hands, rolled clothes or pillows.
3. Cover an open wound with a clean cloth. Press around a bone end, never on it.
4. Put a cold pack wrapped in cloth on the area for up to 20 minutes.
5. Check below the injury: fingers or toes should be warm and pink, and the person should feel your touch.

## Splint only if you must move them

1. Use something stiff: a board, a stick, rolled newspaper or cardboard.
2. Pad it with cloth.
3. Tie it to the limb above and below the injury, not over it.
4. Ties should be firm but not tight. Re-check fingers or toes every 15 minutes.

For an arm, a sling made from a scarf or dupatta holds it against the body.

## Neck or back injury

Suspect it after a fall from height, a road accident, a diving accident or a building collapse.

- Keep the head, neck and body in a straight line.
- **Do not move them unless they are in danger** or not breathing.
- Kneel at the head and hold it still with both hands until help arrives.

## Dislocated joint

Support the joint in the most comfortable position and go to hospital. **Do not try to push it back in.**

## Sprains and strains

1. **Rest** the injured part.
2. **Cold:** ice wrapped in cloth for up to 20 minutes, several times a day for 2 days.
3. **Support:** a firm, not tight, elastic bandage.
4. **Raise** the injured part above the heart while resting.

See a doctor if the person cannot put weight on it, or pain and swelling get worse.

## Do not

- **Do not try to straighten a broken bone.**
- **Do not let anyone massage, pull or set a fracture outside a hospital.** Traditional bonesetting can damage nerves and blood vessels.
- **Do not give food or drink** if the person may need an operation.
