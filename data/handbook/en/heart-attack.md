# Heart attack

**Call 999 first if** someone has chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, especially with sweating, breathlessness, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back.

## Signs

- Pressure, squeezing, heaviness or pain in the centre of the chest. It may come and go.
- Pain spreading to one or both arms, the jaw, neck, back or upper stomach.
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, vomiting, light-headedness.
- Women, older people and people with diabetes may have milder or unusual signs: tiredness, breathlessness or indigestion-like pain.

Do not dismiss it as "gas" or acidity. If in doubt, treat it as a heart attack.

## What to do

1. Call 999 and say "possible heart attack".
2. Help the person sit down, half-sitting with knees bent and back supported is often most comfortable. Keep them still.
3. Loosen tight clothing.
4. **Aspirin:** if they are an adult, not allergic to aspirin, have no bleeding problem, and have not been told by a doctor to avoid it, give one 300 mg aspirin to chew slowly. If unsure, ask the 999 operator or a doctor.
5. If they have their own heart medicine, such as a spray or tablet under the tongue, help them take it as prescribed.
6. Stay with them, keep them calm, and be ready to start CPR.
7. Go to hospital, by ambulance if possible. In Dhaka, the National Institute of Cardiovascular Diseases (NICVD) in Sher-e-Bangla Nagar and large hospitals treat heart attacks around the clock.

## If they collapse

Check breathing. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR at once and send someone for an AED.

## Do not

- **Do not let them walk, climb stairs or drive themselves to hospital.**
- **Do not give food or drink** other than the aspirin.
- **Do not wait for the pain to pass** before calling for help.

## Lower the risk

Control blood pressure, diabetes and cholesterol, stop smoking and tobacco, eat less salt and oily food, and be active every day.
