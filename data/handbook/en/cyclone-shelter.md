# Cyclone shelters and staying safe in a storm

**Call 999 first if** someone is injured, trapped or in danger. **Go to the shelter when you are told to, even at night. Do not wait for the water.** In cyclones, most deaths come from the storm surge, which rises very fast.

## Before the cyclone

1. Know your nearest cyclone shelter (often a school or multi-purpose building) and the way there. Ask your CPP volunteer, union parishad or ward office if you are not sure.
2. Pack your go-bag: documents, water, dry food, ORS, medicines, torch, power bank, cash, clothes, sanitary items and baby food.
3. Tie down tin roofs and bring loose things inside.
4. Store drinking water in covered containers.
5. Move livestock to a raised mound (killa) or high ground. If you cannot, untie them so they can escape.

## When to go

- Signals 5 to 7 (2 flags): get ready. Send older people, children, pregnant women and people with disabilities first.
- Signals 8 to 10 (3 flags and siren): go to the shelter **now**.
- If officials or volunteers tell you to go earlier, go.

## Leaving home

1. Switch off electricity and gas.
2. Take your go-bag and documents.
3. Walk together as a family and hold children's hands.
4. Do not take short cuts through water or along embankments.

## At the shelter

- Give your name to the shelter managers or volunteers, so family members can find you.
- Women, girls, older people and people with disabilities have the right to safe space and separate toilets. Report harassment to volunteers or 999; call 109 for violence against women and children.
- Keep children close, and agree a meeting spot inside.
- Drink safe water, use ORS for diarrhoea, keep food covered.
- Share chargers, and set Harkara mesh to Saver so phones last longer.
- Stay until officials say it is safe to leave.

## If you cannot reach a shelter

- Go to the strongest concrete building nearby, on an upper floor, away from windows.
- Stay away from tin roofs, trees, poles and walls that could fall.

## The eye of the storm

The wind may suddenly stop when the centre of the cyclone passes over. **It will come back, often stronger, from the other direction.** Stay in shelter until the official all-clear.

## After the cyclone

- Watch out for fallen wires, broken trees, damaged buildings and snakes.
- Drink only boiled or treated water.
- Check on neighbours. Report injured and missing people to volunteers or 999.
- Do not go to the shore or river banks to look at the damage.
