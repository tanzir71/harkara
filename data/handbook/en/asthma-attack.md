# Asthma attack

**Call 999 first if** the reliever inhaler does not help, the person is too breathless to speak, their lips turn blue, they are exhausted, or they collapse.

## Signs

- Wheezing, coughing, a tight chest.
- Short of breath; unable to finish sentences.
- Using neck and shoulder muscles to breathe.
- Anxiety. In children: stomach ache, not wanting to play or eat.

## What to do

1. Help the person sit up straight, leaning slightly forward. **Do not lie them down.**
2. Stay calm and keep them calm. Loosen tight clothing.
3. Help them use their **reliever inhaler** (usually blue), with a spacer if they have one: **1 puff every 30 to 60 seconds, up to 10 puffs.**
4. If they feel worse at any time, or are not better after 10 puffs, **call 999**.
5. If help has not come in 10 minutes, give up to 10 more puffs in the same way.
6. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR.

## No spacer?

Make one from a clean 500 ml plastic bottle: cut a hole in the bottom to fit the inhaler mouthpiece, and let the person breathe in and out through the bottle's neck after each puff.

## Triggers to avoid

- Smoke from cooking fires, mosquito coils, cigarettes and burning rubbish.
- Dust and winter air pollution in Dhaka: wear a mask and stay indoors on bad-air days.
- Cold air, strong smells, dust mites, pets and pollen.
- Colds and flu.

## Do not

- **Do not leave them alone.**
- **Do not give sleeping pills or cough syrup** during an attack.
- **Do not wait** if they are getting worse.

## If you have asthma

- Always carry your reliever inhaler.
- Use your preventer inhaler as prescribed, even when you feel well.
- Ask your doctor for a written asthma action plan.
- In smoke or dust from a fire or collapse, cover your nose and mouth and move away early.
