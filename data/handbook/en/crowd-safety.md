# Crowd safety

**Call 999 first if** people have fallen, are being crushed or cannot breathe, or a crowd is surging out of control.

Crowd crushes can happen wherever large numbers of people gather: concerts, festivals, stadiums, train stations and launch terminals at Eid, relief or ticket distributions, markets, exam centres, religious gatherings and rallies. They happen when too many people are packed into too little space, often near entrances, exits, stairs and narrow passages.

## Warning signs

- You cannot move your arms freely, or you are pressed against people on all sides.
- The crowd starts to move in waves that push you off your feet.
- People are shouting that they cannot breathe.
- People are being pushed against barriers, walls or gates.
- Someone falls, and the people around cannot step back.

**Leave as soon as the crowd starts to feel too tight, before it becomes dangerous.**

## Before you go

- Note the exits and an open meeting point, and agree it with your group.
- Arrive early or late to avoid the peak.
- Keep children close. Write your phone number on a child's arm or put it in their pocket.
- Charge your phone, and wear flat shoes that stay on.

## In a dense crowd

1. Stay calm. Panic spreads.
2. Stay near the **edges** of the crowd, but away from walls, barriers, gates and pillars, where pressure builds.
3. **Protect your chest:** hold your arms up in front of your chest, like a boxer, to keep space to breathe.
4. Move **with** the crowd, not against it. Between surges, move diagonally towards the edge, a step at a time.
5. Stay on your feet. **Do not bend down to pick anything up.** Let it go.
6. Avoid stairs, ramps, bridges and narrow lanes when the crowd is pushing.
7. Do not climb walls, gates, poles or train roofs.

## If you fall

1. Try to get up at once.
2. If you cannot, lie on your side, curl up, and protect your head with your arms. This keeps space for your chest to breathe.
3. Get up as soon as there is a gap. Ask people nearby to help lift you.

## Helping others

- Help people who fall to get up quickly.
- Pass simple messages backwards through the crowd: "Stop pushing!", "Move back!", "Person down!" Ask others to pass them on.
- If you are at the back, stop moving forward and step back. Pushing from behind is what crushes people at the front.

## After a crush

- Check the breathing of anyone who was crushed. Start CPR if needed.
- People with chest injuries may breathe more easily sitting up.
- Send people to the agreed meeting point to find their group.

## Organisers and volunteers

- Plan separate entries and exits, with enough space and barriers that do not trap people.
- Do not hand out food, cash or tickets from a single point into a dense crowd.
- Keep emergency routes clear, have first aiders ready, and use loudspeakers to stop entry or open extra exits.
