# Seizures (fits, convulsions)

**Call 999 first if** a seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, another starts soon after, it is the person's first seizure, they are injured, pregnant or diabetic, it happened in water, or they do not wake up properly afterwards.

During a seizure a person may fall, go stiff, jerk, bite their tongue, drool or wet themselves. Breathing may look strange for a short time. Most seizures stop on their own within 1 to 3 minutes.

## During the seizure

1. Stay calm. Note the time it started.
2. Move hard or sharp objects away. Move the person only if they are in danger: road, fire, water, stairs.
3. Cushion the head with something soft, such as a folded jacket.
4. Loosen anything tight around the neck.
5. Keep onlookers back and protect the person's privacy.

## After the jerking stops

1. Roll them onto their side in the recovery position to keep the airway clear.
2. Check their breathing.
3. Talk calmly. They may be confused, sleepy or upset for a while.
4. Stay with them until they are fully awake. Give no food or drink until then.

## Do not

- **Do not hold them down** or try to stop the movements.
- **Do not put anything in their mouth**: no spoon, finger, cloth or water. A person cannot swallow their tongue.
- **Do not make them smell a shoe, onion or anything else.**
- **Do not pour water on them.**

## Fever seizures in young children

1. Protect them as above, and lay them on their side.
2. Remove extra clothes and blankets.
3. **Do not put the child in cold water** during the seizure.
4. Call 999 if it lasts over 5 minutes. Afterwards, see a doctor to find the cause of the fever.

## Seizure in water

Support the head above water. Bring them out when the jerking stops and check their breathing. They need a hospital check even if they seem fine.

## Diabetes

A seizure in someone with diabetes may be from low blood sugar. When they are awake and can swallow safely, give them something sweet.
