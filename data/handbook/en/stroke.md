# Stroke: BE FAST

**Call 999 first** at any sign of a stroke. Every minute counts: treatment works best in the first few hours.

A stroke happens when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked, or a blood vessel in the brain bursts.

## BE FAST

- **B, Balance:** sudden loss of balance or coordination, dizziness, trouble walking.
- **E, Eyes:** sudden loss or blurring of sight in one or both eyes, or double vision.
- **F, Face:** ask them to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
- **A, Arms:** ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down or feel weak?
- **S, Speech:** is their speech slurred or strange? Can they repeat a simple sentence?
- **T, Time:** call 999 now. Note the time the signs started, or when they were last seen well.

Other signs: sudden severe headache, sudden numbness on one side, sudden confusion.

## What to do

1. Call 999 and say "stroke".
2. Note the time the signs started. Doctors will ask.
3. If they are awake, let them lie or sit with head and shoulders slightly raised. Keep them still and comfortable.
4. If unresponsive but breathing, put them in the recovery position.
5. Reassure them. They may understand you even if they cannot speak.
6. Check their breathing. Start CPR if they stop breathing normally.
7. Go to a hospital that treats strokes. In Dhaka, the National Institute of Neurosciences and Hospital at Agargaon specialises in stroke, and large hospitals have emergency stroke care.

## Do not

- **Do not give food, drink or medicine, including aspirin.** A stroke can be a bleed, and swallowing may not be safe.
- **Do not wait to see if it gets better.** Even if the signs go away within minutes (a "mini-stroke"), go to hospital the same day. A major stroke may follow.
- **Do not let them sleep it off.**

## Lower the risk

Control blood pressure, diabetes and cholesterol. Stop smoking and chewing tobacco such as jorda and gul. Eat less salt, move every day, and take prescribed medicines regularly.
