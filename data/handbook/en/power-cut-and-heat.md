# Power cuts and heat

**Call 999 first if** someone collapses in the heat, several people feel ill with headache, dizziness or vomiting near a generator or stove (carbon monoxide), or there is a fire.

Load-shedding and outages are most common in summer, when the heat is worst. Plan for both.

## Generators and carbon monoxide

Generators, charcoal stoves and fuel engines give off carbon monoxide (CO), a gas you cannot see or smell. It kills.

- **Never run a generator inside a home, shop, garage, basement, stairwell or under a closed shutter.**
- Keep it outside, at least 6 metres from doors, windows and vents.
- Do not cook with charcoal or burn kerosene in a closed room.
- Signs of CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, confusion. Several people feeling ill at once in one place is a warning.
- If you suspect CO, get everyone into fresh air at once, open doors and windows, and call 999.

## Fuel and fire safety

- Turn the generator off and let it cool before refuelling. Store fuel in proper containers, away from living spaces and children.
- Do not leave candles, kupi or kerosene lamps burning unattended, near curtains, or where children can knock them over. A torch or rechargeable lamp is safer.
- Switch off appliances during an outage, leaving one light on, so a surge does not damage them or start a fire when power returns.
- Have IPS and battery units installed by a qualified electrician, in an airy place, not in a bedroom or by an exit.

## Staying cool without power

- In the day, close curtains on the sunny side. At night, open windows for a through-breeze.
- Wet a cloth and put it on the back of the neck; sponge the body with water.
- Drink water regularly, and ORS if you sweat a lot.
- Rest in the coolest room, usually on the lowest floor.
- Check on babies, older people and anyone sick. See "Heat stroke, heat exhaustion and dehydration".

## Food safety

- Keep fridge and freezer doors shut. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours; a full freezer for about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24 hours.
- Throw away meat, fish, milk, cooked food and cut fruit that has been warm for more than 2 hours. **When in doubt, throw it out.**
- Cook only what you can eat at one meal. Do not taste food to test it.

## Phone battery

- Charge phones and power banks whenever the power comes back.
- Lower screen brightness and close apps you do not need, especially video and maps.
- Set Harkara's Bluetooth mesh to **Saver** (it listens 1 minute in every 5). For very long outages, turn on **Lite mode**. Use **Event** mode only when you need to relay a lot of messages.
- Use airplane mode when you do not need the network, and check messages at set times.
- Keep the phone out of the sun.

## Medicines and devices

- Some medicines, such as insulin, need to stay cool. Ask your pharmacist how to store them in a power cut.
- If someone at home uses oxygen or a nebuliser, plan a backup power source or know where to go.
