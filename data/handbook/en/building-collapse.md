# Building collapse and being trapped

**Call 999 first.** Give the address, a landmark, how many people may be inside, and which part of the building fell.

Buildings can collapse after earthquakes, fires, gas explosions or floods, or because of faults in how they were built. Warning signs: new cracks in columns or beams, cracking noises, floors or walls shifting, plaster falling, and doors and windows that suddenly jam.

## If you see warning signs

1. Leave the building at once and warn everyone. Do not go back for belongings.
2. Report it to the owner, the city corporation, RAJUK and the Fire Service.
3. **Do not go back inside until engineers say it is safe**, even if you are told to return to work.

## If you are trapped

1. Stay calm. Save your energy and air.
2. **Do not light a match or lighter.** There may be a gas leak.
3. Cover your nose and mouth with cloth against dust.
4. Move as little as possible, so you do not stir up dust or shift the rubble.
5. **Tap on a pipe or wall** in groups of three, then pause and listen. Rescuers listen for sounds.
6. Blow a whistle if you have one. **Shout only as a last resort**: shouting uses energy and makes you breathe in dust.
7. If your phone works, call 999 or send a Harkara SOS with your location. Turn the screen off between tries to save battery.
8. Use a torch or phone light only to signal or to check injuries.
9. Press on any bleeding wound to slow it.

## If people are trapped and you are outside

1. Call 999 and keep the area clear for rescuers.
2. **Do not climb on the rubble or pull out beams.** You can cause another collapse.
3. Help people trapped lightly at the edges only if it is safe.
4. Ask for silence, listen for voices and tapping, and tell rescuers where they come from.
5. Turn off gas and electricity to the site if you can do it safely.
6. Give first aid to people brought out: breathing, bleeding, shock, broken bones.

## Crush injuries

A person trapped for a long time under heavy weight can become suddenly very ill when freed. Let trained rescuers and doctors manage the release. Keep the person warm and calm, and get them to hospital fast.

## Dust

Concrete dust harms the lungs. Wear a mask or cloth over your nose and mouth near a collapse site.
