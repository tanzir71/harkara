# Go-bag and family plan

**In an emergency, call 999 first.** Prepare this before you need it.

A go-bag is a bag you can grab in one minute when you must leave home. Keep it near the door. Each adult can carry one; children can carry a small one.

## Documents (in a waterproof bag)

- Copies of NID cards, birth certificates and passports.
- Land and house papers, bank and mobile banking details.
- Prescriptions, medical records, blood groups and allergies.
- Recent photos of family members, to help find anyone who gets lost.
- A paper list of key numbers: 999, family, neighbours, doctor, school.

## Water and food for 3 days

- Bottled water or a clean container, plus water purification tablets.
- Dry food: chira, muri, biscuits, dates, gur, peanuts, powdered milk, baby food.
- A cup, a spoon and a small knife.

## Health

- ORS packets for several days.
- Regular medicines for 1 to 2 weeks (blood pressure, diabetes, asthma, epilepsy), and paracetamol.
- A first aid kit: bandages, gauze, tape, antiseptic, scissors, gloves.
- Zinc tablets if you have young children.
- Sanitary pads, nappies, soap, toothbrush, hand sanitiser and masks.
- Spare glasses and hearing-aid batteries.

## Light and power

- A torch with spare batteries, or a rechargeable lamp.
- A fully charged power bank and charging cables.
- A small battery radio, if you have one.

## Other useful things

- **A whistle.** Three blasts mean "help". A whistle carries further than a voice and saves your energy.
- Cash in small notes. ATMs and mobile banking may stop working.
- A change of clothes, a light blanket, and a raincoat or plastic sheet.
- Rope, plastic bags, and matches in a waterproof bag (for outdoor use only, never near a gas leak).
- Spare house keys.
- For babies: food, bottles, nappies and a sling. For older people and people with disabilities: walking aids and spare batteries for devices.

## Check the bag twice a year

For example at the start of the monsoon and of winter: replace water, food and medicines near expiry, recharge the power bank, and update phone numbers.

## Family plan

1. **Meeting points:** one near home, one outside your area.
2. **Contact outside Dhaka:** a relative in another district whom everyone tells that they are safe.
3. **Jobs:** who picks up children from school, who helps older relatives, who takes the go-bag.
4. **Ways out:** two ways out of your home and building. Know where the stairs and exits lead.
5. **Mains:** everyone over 12 should know how to switch off electricity, gas and water.
6. **Children:** teach them their full name, parents' names and phone numbers, their address, and how to dial 999.
7. **Harkara:** install it on every family phone before you need it, add each other as contacts, practise a check-in and sharing a location card, and set the mesh to Saver.
8. **Practise** twice a year: grab the bag, walk to the meeting point, and time it.

## Neighbourhood and campus

- Know your neighbours, especially those who live alone or need help.
- Set up a building or hall group in Harkara, and find out if there is a neighbourhood node nearby, or set one up with an old phone.
- Learn first aid and CPR. The Fire Service, the Red Crescent and many organisations run courses.
