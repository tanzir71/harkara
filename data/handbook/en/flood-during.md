# During a flood

**Call 999 first if** someone is trapped, swept away, injured or in danger. If calls do not connect, send a Harkara SOS.

## Moving water

- **Do not walk, swim, drive or ride a rickshaw through moving water.** About 15 cm (ankle to mid-shin) of fast water can knock an adult down. About 30 cm can float a car or CNG.
- If you must walk through still water, test the depth with a stick and feel for holes, open drains and manholes.
- Stay away from river banks, embankments and bridges in high water. They can collapse.
- If you are swept away, float on your back, feet pointing downstream, and steer towards the bank.
- If your vehicle is caught in rising water, get out and move to higher ground.

## Electricity

- Switch off the power at the main switch **before** water reaches the sockets, if you can do it from a dry place.
- **Do not touch switches, plugs or appliances while standing in water or with wet hands.**
- Keep well away from fallen wires, electric poles, transformers and meter boxes in water. Report them to 999.

## Safe drinking water

Flood water carries sewage and chemicals. Drink only safe water, and use it for brushing teeth, washing food and making ORS too.

1. **Boil:** bring clear water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute. Let it cool, and store it covered.
2. **Purification tablets:** use as the packet says, usually one tablet for a set amount of water, then wait 30 minutes before drinking.
3. If the water is cloudy, let it settle and pour off the clear part, or strain it through clean folded cloth, before boiling or treating.
4. Pour from a clean covered container. Do not dip cups into it.

Do not drink from a tubewell that went under water until it has been cleaned and pumped out.

## Snakes and animals

- Snakes, insects and rats move to dry places: beds, shelves, roofs, piles of clothes.
- Use a torch at night. Shake out bedding and shoes. Sleep on a raised bed under a net.
- If bitten, see "Snake bite". **No tourniquet, no cutting.** Get to antivenom fast.

## Health

- Wash hands with soap and safe water before eating.
- Cover cuts before going into water; keep wounds clean and dry.
- Start ORS early for diarrhoea. See "Diarrhoea and cholera".
- Dry your feet well and change into dry clothes when you can.

## Stay informed and in touch

- Keep your phone in a plastic bag. Save battery and set Harkara mesh to Saver.
- Listen to the radio, or call 1090, for warnings.
- Let family know where you are with a Harkara check-in.

## If you must leave

1. Switch off electricity and gas.
2. Take your go-bag and documents.
3. Follow the route given by officials or volunteers to a shelter or high ground.
4. Help older people, children, pregnant women and people with disabilities.
5. Do not return until officials say it is safe.

## If you are trapped

- Go to the highest floor or the roof. **Do not climb into a closed space under the roof where rising water can trap you.**
- Signal for help: wave a bright cloth, blow a whistle, and at night use the Harkara torch strobe or Morse SOS.
- Send a Harkara SOS with your location.
