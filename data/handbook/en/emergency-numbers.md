# Emergency numbers (Bangladesh)

**Call 999 first** for any danger to life: fire, a serious injury, someone not breathing, a crime happening now, a building collapse, or people trapped in water.

All numbers below were checked against official or news sources in November 2026. Numbers can change; the sources are listed in the app's handbook information.

## 999: National Emergency Service

- Police, Fire Service and ambulance through one number.
- Free from any mobile or landline, 24 hours a day.
- On most Android phones you can dial 999 from the lock screen with "Emergency call".
- Keep the call short and clear. See "How to use this handbook" for what to say.

## Fire Service and Civil Defence

- **16163**: Fire Service central control room hotline.
- **02-223355555**: Fire Service headquarters control room, Dhaka.
- **01730-336699**: Fire Service central monitoring cell (mobile).
- 999 also connects you to the Fire Service. Use these numbers if 999 is busy, or to report a fire risk.

## Disaster and weather warnings

- **1090**: free recorded warnings from the Department of Disaster Management (DDM): weather, cyclone signals, river levels and floods. Follow the voice menu for your topic.

## Health advice

- **16263**: Shastho Batayon, the government health call centre. Doctors give advice by phone, day and night.
  - In November 2026 this service stopped for some days because of a contract problem, and the health ministry said it would restart. If nobody answers, call 999 or go to the nearest hospital.

## Protection and support

- **109**: National Helpline Centre for Violence against Women and Children. Free, 24 hours. Advice, referral, and help to reach police, shelter, medical care or legal aid.
- **1098**: Child Helpline. Free, 24 hours. For a child in danger or distress, or an adult worried about a child.
- **16699**: National Legal Aid helpline (National Legal Aid Services Organisation). Free legal advice. This number replaced the old 16430 in November 2025.

## Government information

- **333**: National information and service helpline: government services, offices, complaints and local information. A small call charge applies (about 60 paisa a minute). From a landline or from abroad: 09666-789333.

## Hospitals

- Hospital phone numbers change often, so they are not listed here.
- When you have internet, save the emergency number of your nearest government hospital and one private hospital, in your phone and on paper.
- For a life-threatening emergency, go straight to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call 999.

## Keep the numbers ready

- Write 999 and two family numbers on paper, in your wallet and in your go-bag.
- Teach children how to dial 999 and how to say their address.
- Save these numbers in your phone contacts now, while you have time.
