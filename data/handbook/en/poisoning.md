# Poisoning

**Call 999 first if** the person is drowsy, unresponsive, having a seizure or trouble breathing, or has swallowed a lot of medicine, kerosene, insecticide, rat poison, acid, bleach or something unknown.

## Swallowed poison

1. Find out what they took, how much and when. Keep the bottle, packet or plant to show the doctor.
2. If they are awake, wipe the mouth and let them rinse it with water and spit it out.
3. Go to hospital at once, with the container.
4. If they are unresponsive but breathing, put them in the recovery position.
5. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR. If there is poison on their lips, do compression-only CPR.

## Do not

- **Do not make them vomit.** No fingers in the throat, no salt water, no soapy water. Vomit can go into the lungs, and acid or kerosene burns again on the way up.
- **Do not give milk, oil, eggs or other "antidotes"** unless a doctor tells you to.
- **Do not wait for symptoms.** Some poisons, such as a paracetamol overdose, seem harmless at first and damage the liver a day or two later.

## Kerosene, petrol, diesel and thinner

- Children often drink these from soft-drink bottles used for storage.
- The main danger is to the lungs: coughing, choking, fast breathing, fever later.
- Go to hospital even if the child seems well.

## Insecticides and pesticides

Signs: small pupils, sweating, drooling, watery eyes, vomiting, diarrhoea, muscle twitching, weakness, difficulty breathing.

1. Protect yourself with gloves or plastic bags on your hands.
2. Remove soaked clothing and put it in a bag.
3. Wash the skin and hair with soap and plenty of water.
4. Go to hospital urgently with the container. Hospitals have treatment for these poisons.

## On the skin or in the eyes

- Skin: remove clothing and rinse with running water for 20 minutes.
- Eyes: rinse with clean running water for 15 to 20 minutes, holding the lids open.

## Smoke and fumes

Carbon monoxide from generators, charcoal stoves or engines in closed spaces causes headache, dizziness, vomiting and collapse. Get everyone into fresh air, open doors and windows, and call 999 for anyone drowsy or unresponsive.

## Drugged food or drink

If someone becomes suddenly drowsy or confused after food or drink from a stranger on a bus, launch or train, keep them on their side, keep their belongings safe, and get them to hospital.

## Poisoning on purpose

If someone took poison or pills to harm themselves, get them to hospital, stay with them, and do not leave them alone afterwards. Listen without judging, and help them find ongoing support.

## Prevent poisoning

- Lock away medicines, kerosene, acid, bleach, insecticides and rat poison, out of children's reach.
- **Never store kerosene or chemicals in drink bottles.**
- Keep products in their original labelled containers.
- Do not spray insecticide in a closed room with people inside.
