# When there is no network

**Call 999 first if** a life is in danger. An emergency call may get through when data does not. Keep trying, from a different spot if needed.

During floods, storms, power cuts and network outages, mobile data and calls can stop. Harkara is built to keep working.

## How Harkara keeps working

Harkara tries these routes from top to bottom, by itself. The status pill at the top of the screen shows which one is in use.

1. **Internet relays** ("Online"): when you have mobile data or Wi-Fi.
2. **A nearby phone with data** ("Via a nearby phone"): another Harkara phone close by lifts your message onto the internet.
3. **Neighbourhood node** ("On local Wi-Fi"): an old phone on a charger, connected to the hall, building or campus Wi-Fi. It works even when that Wi-Fi has no internet, and can also serve the map and this handbook.
4. **Bluetooth mesh** ("Nearby only"): messages hop from phone to phone, up to 7 hops, with no network at all.
5. **SMS** ("By text message"): Harkara fills in a text in your SMS app. Normal SMS charges apply.
6. **QR code** ("Hand to hand"): show a QR code; someone scans it and carries the message on.
7. **Saved on phone** ("Saved, sends later"): the message waits and goes as soon as a route opens.

Good to know:

- Messages are signed, and chats are encrypted. Phones in the mesh carry private messages but cannot read them.
- The more Harkara phones nearby, the better the mesh. Ask neighbours, classmates and family to install it before an emergency.
- Mesh modes: **Saver** listens 1 minute in every 5 and saves battery. **Event** relays more and uses more battery; use it when many people need to pass messages, for example in a shelter.
- A neighbourhood node needs only an old Android phone, a charger and local Wi-Fi.

## Agree a family plan now

1. **Two meeting points:** one near home (a gate, tree or shop) and one outside your area (a relative's house, school, mosque or field).
2. **One contact outside Dhaka:** in a disaster, a call to another district may work when local lines are busy. Everyone tells that person they are safe.
3. **Check-in times:** for example, everyone sends a Harkara check-in or a text at 8 pm each day.
4. **Who collects whom:** children from school, older relatives, people with disabilities.
5. **Write it on paper** in every go-bag and wallet. Phones die.

## Reaching people without data

- Send a short SMS rather than calling. Texts often get through when calls fail.
- Use a Harkara check-in: one tap says "I got here safe".
- Share a location card with a Plus Code, which works offline.
- Post an Alert in Harkara Nearby about blocked roads, water or help points.
- At night, use the Harkara torch strobe or Morse SOS. Three whistle blasts also mean "help".

## If you hear nothing

- Stay calm. No reply does not mean bad news; networks may simply be down.
- Go to the agreed meeting point at the agreed time.
- Leave a note at home saying where you have gone.
