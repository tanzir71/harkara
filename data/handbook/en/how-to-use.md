# How to use this handbook

**In an emergency, call 999 first.** It is free from any phone, day and night. Read this handbook while you wait for help, or when help cannot reach you yet.

## Works without internet

- Every page is stored on your phone. No signal, data or Wi-Fi is needed.
- Search works offline in Bangla and English. Try simple words such as "bleeding", "burn", "flood" or "রক্ত".
- Change the language in Settings → Language. Both versions give the same advice.
- Open the handbook once now, while you are calm, so you know where things are.

## Harkara SOS

Use SOS when you, or someone near you, is in danger and people nearby need to know.

1. Press and hold the status pill at the top of the screen (the one that says "Online", "Nearby only" and so on) for 3 seconds.
2. A screen opens with **Call 999** at the top. If you can make a call, tap it first.
3. Tap **Send SOS** and confirm.
4. Harkara sends an SOS with your location over every route that is open: internet relays, a nearby phone with data, the neighbourhood node, Bluetooth mesh, and SMS if you allow it.
5. Keep the phone switched on and with you. Replies and offers of help arrive like normal messages.
6. When you are safe, mark the SOS as resolved so people stop searching.

Good to know:

- SOS reaches Harkara users and your contacts. **It does not reach the police, fire service or ambulance. Only a call to 999 does that.**
- Harkara limits how often an SOS can be repeated, so nearby phones are not flooded with alerts.
- Tapping the Harkara logo three times is **Panic wipe**, which erases Harkara's data on the phone. It is not SOS.

## Stay calm, then act

1. Stop. Take three slow breaths: in through the nose, out through the mouth.
2. Look around. Is there fire, traffic, a live wire, rising water or something that could fall? **Do not become the second casualty.**
3. Decide the one thing to do next, and do it. Then the next.
4. Give clear jobs to people around you: "You, call 999." "You, bring clean water." "You, keep the crowd back."
5. Talk to the injured person. Tell them your name and that help is coming.

## What to tell 999

Speak slowly. Answer the questions. Do not hang up until the operator tells you to.

- **Where:** area, road and house number, and a landmark a driver can find: a mosque, school, market, bank, pharmacy or bus stop. Say which floor. Harkara's location card shows a Plus Code you can read out.
- **What happened:** fire, road accident, someone collapsed, someone not breathing, flooding, a damaged building.
- **How many people** are hurt, and whether anyone is trapped, unconscious or bleeding heavily.
- **Dangers:** gas smell, chemicals, live wires, smoke, deep or moving water.
- **Your phone number.** Keep the line free for a call back.

Tips:

- Send someone to the main road to guide the ambulance or fire engine in.
- If you cannot speak safely, ask someone nearby to call for you.
- Write your home address in large letters and keep it next to the phone.

## How the pages are laid out

- The first line tells you when to call 999.
- Numbered steps are in the order to do them.
- **Bold "Do not" lines are warnings.** They matter as much as the steps.
- This handbook is not a doctor. When in doubt, get medical help.
