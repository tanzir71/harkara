# Fainting

**Call 999 first if** the person does not wake within 1 to 2 minutes, is not breathing normally, had chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat or a seizure, was injured in the fall, is pregnant, fainted during exercise, or is over 50 and has never fainted before.

Fainting is a short loss of consciousness because too little blood reaches the brain. Common causes: standing a long time in heat, skipping meals, dehydration, pain, fear, the sight of blood, or standing up too fast.

## Warning signs before a faint

Light-headedness, sweating, pale skin, blurred or narrowing vision, nausea, ringing in the ears.

## If someone feels faint

1. Help them lie down. If they cannot, sit them down and lean them forward.
2. Raise their legs.
3. Loosen tight clothing. Fan them and give them fresh air.
4. When they feel better, sit them up slowly.

## If someone has fainted

1. Lay them on their back.
2. Raise their legs, resting the ankles on a bag, chair or your shoulders.
3. Check that they are breathing normally.
4. Keep people back to give them air.
5. Most people wake within a minute. Help them sit up slowly. Give water or ORS once they are fully awake.
6. If they do not wake: if they are not breathing normally, start CPR; if they are breathing, use the recovery position and call 999.

## Afterwards

- Let them rest 10 to 15 minutes before standing.
- Give food and a drink if they have not eaten.
- Check for injuries from the fall, especially to the head.
- They should see a doctor if it happens again or there is no clear reason.

## Do not

- **Do not sit up or stand a person who has just fainted.**
- **Do not splash water on the face or slap them.**
- **Do not make them smell a shoe, onion or strong chemicals.** It does not help.
- **Do not give a drink to someone who is not fully awake.**
