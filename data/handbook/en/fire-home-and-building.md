# Fire at home and in buildings

**Call 999 first** for any fire you cannot put out within seconds. Get everyone out, then call from outside.

## If there is a fire

1. Shout "Fire!" to warn everyone.
2. Get everyone out. Do not stop for belongings.
3. Close doors behind you to slow the fire.
4. Call 999 once you are outside.
5. **Do not go back in.** Tell firefighters who is still inside, and where.

## Smoke

- Smoke kills more people than flames. Smoke rises; the cleaner air is near the floor.
- **Crawl low** under smoke, with your face close to the floor.
- Cover your nose and mouth with a cloth, wet if possible.
- Before opening a door, touch it and the handle with the back of your hand. **If it is hot, do not open it.** Use another way out.

## Tall buildings

1. Know two ways out from your floor. Keep stairs and fire exits free of storage and locks.
2. **Use the stairs, never the lift.**
3. Go down, not up to the roof, unless fire or smoke blocks the stairs below. Roof doors are often locked.
4. If you cannot get out: go into a room with a window, shut the door, and block the gaps with wet cloth. Signal from the window with a cloth or torch. Call 999 and give your floor and room.
5. **Do not jump.** Wait for firefighters.

## Gas leaks: cylinder (LPG) or gas line

Signs: smell of gas, hissing sound.

1. **Do not switch any light, fan or appliance on or off, and do not light a match or lighter.**
2. Turn off the cylinder regulator or the gas line valve.
3. Open doors and windows wide.
4. Get everyone out, and call 999 or the gas supplier from outside.

Prevent gas fires and explosions:

- Before lighting the stove, especially in the morning or after a long break, open the kitchen windows for a few minutes.
- Turn off burner knobs and the regulator after cooking and at night.
- Check pipes and joints with soapy water: bubbles mean a leak. **Never use a flame to check.**
- Replace old rubber pipes and regulators. Buy cylinders from approved dealers, and keep them upright in an airy place.
- **Do not use the gas stove to dry clothes or heat the room.**

## Electrical fire

1. Switch off the power at the main switch if you can do so safely.
2. **Do not throw water on it while the power is on.**
3. Use a CO2 or dry powder extinguisher, or smother a small fire with a fire blanket.
4. If it spreads, get out and call 999.

Do not overload sockets, replace damaged wires, and do not charge batteries overnight near exits or beds.

## Burning oil in a pan

Turn off the heat and cover the pan with a lid or a damp (not dripping) cloth. Leave it covered for 30 minutes. **Never throw water on burning oil.** It explodes into a fireball.

## Fire extinguisher: PASS

**P**ull the pin. **A**im at the base of the fire. **S**queeze the handle. **S**weep side to side. Keep an exit behind you. If the fire does not go down quickly, leave.

## Clothes on fire: stop, drop and roll

1. **Stop.** Do not run.
2. **Drop** to the ground and cover your face with your hands.
3. **Roll** over and over until the flames are out.

Then cool the burns with water for 20 minutes.

## Prepare

- Smoke alarms on every floor, tested monthly. An extinguisher on each floor, and people who know how to use it.
- Keys to grilles and collapsible gates where everyone can reach them quickly.
- A meeting point outside, and a fire drill with family or colleagues twice a year.
