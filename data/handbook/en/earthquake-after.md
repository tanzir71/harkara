# After an earthquake

**Call 999 first if** someone is trapped or seriously injured, or there is a fire or gas leak.

## Right after the shaking stops

1. Check yourself and people near you for injuries. Stop heavy bleeding first.
2. **Expect aftershocks.** They can come minutes, hours or days later. Drop, cover and hold on each time.
3. Put on shoes to protect your feet from broken glass.
4. If the building is damaged, or you smell gas, leave calmly by the stairs. Do not use the lift.
5. Go to an open space away from buildings, walls and wires, and meet your family there.

## Gas, electricity and fire

- **If you smell gas or hear hissing: do not light matches, lighters or candles, and do not switch anything on or off.** Open windows, turn off the gas valve or cylinder regulator if you can, leave, and report it.
- **Use a torch, not a flame.** The Harkara torch works without a network.
- Switch off the power at the main switch if you see sparks or broken wires, or smell burning.
- Put out small fires quickly. Leave if they spread.

## Check the building

Do not go back inside if you see:

- Cracks in columns or beams, especially diagonal or X-shaped cracks.
- Leaning walls or floors, doors that suddenly jam, broken stairs.
- Sagging roofs or floors, or large pieces of fallen plaster.

Ask an engineer or the authorities to check the building before you move back in.

## Trapped people

- Call 999. Tell them exactly where people are trapped.
- **Do not lift slabs or dig in an unstable building without trained rescuers.** You may cause another collapse.
- Ask everyone to be quiet for a minute, and listen for tapping or voices.
- See "Building collapse and being trapped".

## Staying in touch

- Keep calls short so the network stays free for emergencies. Send texts or Harkara messages instead.
- Send a Harkara check-in so your family knows you are safe.
- If mobile networks are down, Harkara's Bluetooth mesh and neighbourhood node can still carry messages nearby.

## Water and toilets

- Pipes and tanks may be broken or dirty. Drink boiled or treated water.
- Do not flush toilets until you know the sewer pipes are intact.

## Look after each other

- Check on neighbours, especially older people, people with disabilities and people who live alone.
- Children may be frightened. Keep them close and explain simply what happened.
- Do not spread rumours of predicted earthquakes. Nobody can predict the exact time of an earthquake.
