<p><b>No signal? Harkara gets through.</b></p>
<p>Harkara (হরকরা — the old dak runner who carried letters hand to hand) is a neighbourhood and campus app for Dhaka that keeps working during floods, power cuts and everyday network trouble.</p>
<p>Every post, message, task, pin and IOU is a small signed message that phones carry for each other. It goes over whichever route is open: public internet relays, a nearby phone with data, a neighbourhood node on local Wi-Fi, Bluetooth mesh, SMS, a QR code — or it waits on your phone and leaves the moment a route opens.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Nearby</b> — an anonymous local feed (Traffic, Food, Crowd, Stock, Alert, Chatter) where posts fade when they expire.</li>
<li><b>Chats</b> — end-to-end encrypted DMs and groups, plus public area channels and an open "everyone on this Wi-Fi" room.</li>
<li><b>Tasks</b> — local errands with signed steps and an offline handoff code.</li>
<li><b>Khata</b> — IOUs both people sign; settle with a bKash or Nagad TrxID.</li>
<li><b>Me</b> — your keys, offline tools (compass, Qibla, torch, emergency handbook) and optional modules.</li>
</ul>
<p>Beta software: Bluetooth mesh is Beta and real-world radio behaviour is not yet verified on hardware. Nearby phones and an open route are needed; delivery is not guaranteed. In an emergency, call 999 first.</p>
<p>SMS normally opens your SMS app with text prefilled. Direct SMS and the volunteer SMS gateway are optional, off by default and spend SIM credit. Maps and other optional modules depend on availability; map packs are separate downloads.</p>
<p>No sign-up, no phone number, no server, no ads, no tracking. Free software under AGPL-3.0-or-later.</p>
